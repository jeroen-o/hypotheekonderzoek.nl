What regulation requires of mortgage lenders, and the data we provide

An overview by framework: ESG and Pillar 3, the EBA Guidelines, DNB reporting, NHG and the AFM. This is not legal advice; compliance remains your own responsibility.

ESG reporting

Data for ESG reporting on your mortgage portfolio

European rules increasingly require lenders to factor sustainability and climate risks into credit assessment and risk management, and to report on them. For mortgages, the focus is on the collateral: how energy-efficient is the property, and what physical climate risk does it face? This requires reliable market reference data.

E

Environmental

The distribution of energy labels in your new lending compared with the market, and how it is trending. This underpins reporting on the energy efficiency of your collateral, on transition risk, and an indicative estimate of financed CO₂ emissions (PCAF).

S

Social

Access to your credit for first-time buyers, single-person households, older borrowers and women, benchmarked against the market. This helps to substantiate responsible and non-discriminatory lending.

G

Governance

Traceable, aggregated data with a documented methodology, usable as an external reference in your risk management and in your accountability to supervisors.

Regulation

Alignment with the EBA Guidelines on loan origination and monitoring

The EBA Guidelines on loan origination and monitoring (EBA/GL/2020/06) have applied since 30 June 2021. Among other things, they require institutions to monitor portfolios by segment, to take ESG factors into account and to test models for bias. Our insights provide external reference data for those analyses.

National legislation

Wet rapportage hypotheekmarkt DNB (Dutch Mortgage Market Reporting Act)

This Act requires banks, insurers, pension funds and investment institutions (and their managers) to report data on their residential and commercial real estate mortgage loans to De Nederlandsche Bank. The aim is reliable statistics and risk assessments for financial stability. The data are loan-level and cover the loan, the collateral and the borrower, including the energy label, the LTV and the loan-to-income ratio. The data are submitted in pseudonymised form. Exactly which data, which thresholds and how often will be set out in an AMvB (governmental decree). An implementation period of six months will then apply.

What Hypotheekonderzoek.nl™ provides: an independent market reference alongside your own reporting. It allows you to compare your portfolio by age, household and energy label, and your interest rate positions, with the intermediary channel.

Status: the Act amends the Bankwet 1998 (Dutch Bank Act 1998) and has been passed. The Tweede Kamer (House of Representatives) approved it on 11 June 2026 and the Eerste Kamer (Dutch Senate) on 30 June 2026, in both cases as a hamerstuk (adopted without debate or a recorded vote; parliamentary paper 36 846). The Act now awaits promulgation and will enter into force on a date to be set by Royal Decree. Sources: Eerste Kamer and Wetgevingskalender (legislative calendar).

Guarantee

Nationale Hypotheek Garantie (NHG, Dutch National Mortgage Guarantee)

NHG gives the lender security through a guarantee (surety). If a forced sale yields less than the residual debt, the lender can claim on NHG, provided the NHG terms and conditions were met at the time of the offer. With a hybrid valuation, a building survey report is not mandatory for NHG, but a lender may still request one if it wants the property to be structurally inspected.

What Hypotheekonderzoek.nl™ provides: the NHG share per lender and in the market, the split between full and hybrid valuations, how often a building survey report is requested, and the profile of NHG applicants (age, household, energy label) compared with non-NHG applications.

Sources: NHG: guarantee fund structure and NHG: building survey report with a hybrid valuation (both in Dutch).

AFM

Interest-only mortgages: engaging customers

According to the AFM, more than half of Dutch mortgage debt is interest-only, and many of these mortgages mature between 2035 and 2040. The AFM expects lenders to approach at-risk customers, to monitor that they stay on track, and to record which techniques work. Ultimate responsibility lies with the lender, which works with advisers to this end. As early as 2016, DNB identified a risk group: mortgage holders over 50 with a largely interest-only mortgage and a high LTV.

What Hypotheekonderzoek.nl™ provides: visibility of what happens in the advice process with customers who hold an interest-only mortgage. You see how often they seek advice, which solutions are modelled (repaying, converting, refinancing, increasing the loan), which repayment type they switch to, and how this differs by age band and by advisory firm. This lets you measure whether your customer engagement approach works in the intermediary channel.

Sources: AFM: interest-only mortgages and DNB: bank mortgage portfolios under scrutiny (2016) (both in Dutch).
